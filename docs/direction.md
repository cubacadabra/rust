Yes. **You are now pointed in the right direction.** I would keep going from this branch rather than rethink the whole character system again.

The important qualification is: getting the *person* less creepy does not automatically make every body type less creepy. What you’ve corrected is the **design philosophy** that should now be applied across all of them:

* simple, friendly faces first
* strong readable silhouette before detail
* real hand/foot/hair shapes instead of generic rounded blocks
* clothing that wraps a body rather than looking bolted onto it
* restrained materials instead of “everything is shiny plastic”
* appealing idle pose before fancy animation
* animation that preserves weight/contact instead of just moving joints
* asymmetry and larger authored forms where they add personality

That philosophy scales very well to the other body types.

The biggest thing I would guard against now is **over-generalizing the human solution**. A unicorn, robot, creature, childlike avatar, etc. should not just be the same rounded-body recipe with different attachments. Each body family should get its own silhouette language.

For example, I’d eventually structure the art direction roughly like this:

| Body family           | What should carry the personality                                    |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Human/person          | hair, face, clothes, hands, shoes, stance                            |
| Unicorn/equine        | head/neck silhouette, ears, mane, tail, hooves, body proportions     |
| Small/cute creature   | head/body ratio, oversized features, compressed limbs                |
| Large/strong creature | shoulders, stance width, hands/feet, slower motion                   |
| Magical/abstract      | silhouette + controlled magical accents, not extra facial complexity |

The **face grammar can remain shared**, though. That is a strength. Two dark eyes plus a small mouth can work across a huge range of characters if the surrounding proportions are good.

And that is actually one of the most encouraging things about your Roblox screenshots: the face itself is almost comically primitive. The appeal comes from the whole character.

So you do **not** need Pixar facial rigs to reach “wow.”

You need:

**great silhouette + great proportions + great hair/clothes/accessories + believable motion + excellent art direction.**

That is achievable with what you are building.

### Where I think “wow” comes later

Once the current fundamentals are solid, there are several layers that could push Cubacadabra beyond merely “not creepy.”

**Secondary motion.** Hair locks, ponytails, ears, tails, loose hoodie strings, coat hems. Very restrained movement makes a character suddenly feel alive.

**Excellent locomotion.** Feet actually plant, hips respond, arms counter-swing naturally, body leans into acceleration, stops have weight. Kids may not consciously identify this, but they absolutely feel it.

**Distinct materials.** Hair should read differently from cotton, denim, rubber soles, skin, metal, fuzz, etc. Not photorealistically—just enough for the brain to categorize them instantly.

**Layered clothing silhouettes.** A hoodie over a T-shirt should genuinely change the outline. Big boots should affect stance. A skirt, jacket, backpack, cape, helmet, etc. should materially change the character.

**Strong hairstyles.** This is potentially huge. Ten really appealing hairstyles can create more perceived character variety than fifty subtle facial variations.

**Expressive posing.** A shy idle, confident idle, goofy run, dramatic jump, relaxed lean, wave, dance. The body becomes part of the character’s expression.

**Customization that preserves quality.** This is ultimately where your architecture matters. You want lots of combinations without allowing combinations to destroy silhouette, clip badly, or make the character uncanny.

And then there is one particularly important opportunity for Cubacadabra:

### Your own visual signature

You do not want to end at “Roblox but nicer.”

The cube/magic theme gives you a way out.

I would keep the base characters approachable and recognizable, then introduce the Cubacadabra identity through **subtle geometry and magic**:

slightly faceted curves, chunky forms, carefully squared-off details, unusual seams, magical reactions during emotes/jumps/equipment changes, tasteful glowing accents, shape transformations.

In other words:

**normal enough to instantly like, distinctive enough to instantly recognize.**

That is a much stronger direction than making the normal anatomy itself strange.

And I think this is the central lesson from the creepy versions: you were asking the *body construction* to carry too much of the originality.

Let the **character be friendly first**.

Let **Cubacadabra's world, styling, magic, materials, accessories, animation, and shape language** make it original.

If you continue the current correction with that rule, I absolutely think the architecture can support characters that eventually make the present Roblox avatar look crude by comparison. The hard part from here is less “can the renderer do it?” and increasingly **having ruthless taste about which shapes actually ship.**
